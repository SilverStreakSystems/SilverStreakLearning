# Burden model

Any governed workflow can be made rigorous by pushing effort onto the people the product
serves. That is a failure, not a trade-off.

## Burden assessment

Each stream records a burden assessment naming:

- who bears the additional effort (educator, family, student, operator, or vendor),
- what the effort actually is, in concrete terms,
- what is offset or removed elsewhere to pay for it,
- whether the burden falls on a person the product exists to serve,
- the alternative considered and why it was not taken.

## Rules

1. **New burden on a served person requires an offset.** If nothing is removed, the stream is
   recorded as adding net burden and that appears on its surface.
2. **Duplicate entry is refused.** If a structured source already holds the information, the
   workflow reads it rather than asking again.
3. **Burden is measured against the person, not the system.** A step that is cheap for the
   enterprise and expensive for an educator is expensive.
4. **Low-incidence and small-district contexts are not edge cases.** A workflow that only works
   with a dedicated administrative team does not work.
5. **Burden findings return to the learning loop** rather than being accepted as the cost of
   governance.
