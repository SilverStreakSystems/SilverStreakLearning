## 1. Document identity and intent

A source-verifiable, non-duplicative research **specification** to be pasted into Claude (web
research enabled) to run a K-12 research program for SSLS 3.0: mine, normalize, compare, and
crosswalk Massachusetts academic frameworks with multilingual-learner, academic-language, SEL,
justice, accessibility, and special-population guidance. It produces research artifacts only —
never product functionality, educational decisions, or implementation/validation claims.

Front-matter boundary (page 1): Owner Shelby Russell; private development workspace;
founder/developer-only user; synthetic, No-PII data; not released, not production-authorized, not
district-authorized. **Nothing is inherited** — no technology, validation, role, route, gate,
package, claim, or canon.

Terminology corrections (page 2): use "Learning for Justice" unless a verified source names
another organization; use **CALP, not CALPS**; treat BICS/CALP as a conceptual lens, never as
standards, labels, or eligibility rules.

### Research method sections

- **§4 "Mine" definition** — discover current official materials; verify authority and currency;
  inventory; extract atomic units preserving codes, grade spans, domains, qualifiers, citations;
  normalize without erasing source language; record ambiguity; map only with evidence. Never rely
  on snippets/secondary summaries when a primary source exists.
- **§5 Source hierarchy** — (1) enacted law/regulation, (2) current official DESE standards and
  guidance, (3) official framework publishers (WIDA, CASEL, CAST, Learning for Justice),
  (4) peer-reviewed scholarship, (5) official implementation resources, (6) reputable secondary
  synthesis, (7) labeled practitioner examples. Authority ≠ conceptual superiority; record scope,
  jurisdiction, status, intended use separately.
- **§6 Mandatory source families** — all current MA curriculum frameworks (ELA, Math, STE, HSS,
  DLCS, Health/PE, Arts, World Languages, CVTE, others DESE lists); WIDA ELD framework + key
  language uses, expectations, proficiency descriptors; DESE NGESL Toolkit, ELE Blueprint, ELE
  guidance; SLIFE/newcomer/former-EL/dual-identification guidance; CASEL framework +
  academic-integration resources; Learning for Justice Social Justice Standards; BICS/CALP
  original and later scholarship; CAST UDL Guidelines; MA MTSS and inclusive practice. Expand the
  corpus only for a documented gap.
- **§7 Special populations** — MLs, former ELs, newcomers, SLIFE, EL+disability, IEP, 504,
  homelessness/foster/migration/displacement/instability, economically disadvantaged/Title I,
  advanced learners, dyslexia and language-based needs, tiered supports. Never collapse groups;
  model intersectionality; classify guidance by function (access, eligibility, service delivery,
  assessment, accommodations, family engagement, civil rights, instructional design).
- **§8 Grade model** — preserve exact source spans **and** a normalized K-2 / 3-5 / 6-8 / 9-12
  view; never force a source into a band; tag applicability across content area, language domain,
  setting, program model, population, educator role, evidence type; identify progressions and
  discontinuities without inventing standards.
- **§9 Extraction method** — source-discovery plan and authority-register schema first; verify
  before extracting; page numbers for PDFs, stable anchors + retrieval dates for web; preserve
  exact codes; short quotes only when wording is essential; stable IDs; full provenance;
  researcher inference in a separate field.
- **§10 Synthesis** — convergences, complements, tensions, omissions, incompatible assumptions;
  implications for planning, instruction, observable evidence, scaffolding, assessment, family
  partnership, later requirements. Research findings never become product requirements without a
  labeled human-review step.
- **§11 Copyright** — no wholesale reproduction; prefer citations, identifiers, structured
  metadata, original synthesis; flag content requiring permission/licensing/link-only handling.
- **§12 Evidence quality** — every claim traceable; current vs archived/superseded/draft/legacy
  distinguished; confidence assigned; broken links and version conflicts flagged; never represent
  guidance as law, examples as requirements, lenses as standards, or crosswalk judgments as
  official equivalencies.
- **§13 Execution phases** — (1) questions/terminology/hierarchy/schemas, (2) discover-verify-
  deduplicate, (3) extract atomic evidence, (4) normalize, (5) crosswalk, (6) synthesize,
  (7) quality gates + human-review queue. Per-phase reporting of work, counts, exclusions,
  unresolved items, next bounded phase.
- **§15 Stop rules** — pause for: unverifiable source; design-changing authority conflict;
  access/licensing limits; legal-advice territory; PII appearing; consequential educational
  decision requested; local policy needed. Mark gaps `[LOCAL INPUT REQUIRED]`.
- **§20 BICS/CALP safeguard** — conceptual lens only; never fixed stages, diagnostic labels,
  ability categories, or reasons to delay grade-level access; include critiques and later
  scholarship; examine relevance beyond multilingual learners.
- **§21 Conflict/currency protocol** — preserve both positions; identify jurisdiction/authority;
  classify the difference (law, policy, framework design, scholarship, date, local discretion);
  route unresolved interpretation to human review. Distinguish WIDA 2020 framework from later
  support resources; current vs archived DESE guidance; legal obligations vs nonbinding guidance;
  evidence vs inference; research conclusions vs SSLS proposals.

### Data-structure sections

- **§16 Special-population matrix** — per population: source + issuer, version/status, definition
  and eligibility boundaries, required/recommended/illustrative practices, instructional-access,
  language-development, assessment/accommodation, family-engagement implications, intersections,
  SSLS 3.0 design considerations, misuse/labeling/deficit-framing risks.
- **§17 Authority register** — one row per canonical source: Source ID, exact title, issuer,
  jurisdiction, framework/program, version, publication/revision dates, effective date, official
  URL, source type, authority level, grade span, subject/domain, population applicability, status,
  pages/sections, key concepts, limitations, verification date. One canonical copy per source;
  dedupe by normalized title, issuer, version, URL, file hash, content fingerprint; alternates
  stored on the same record.
- **§18 Atomic evidence register** — one row per independently traceable statement: Evidence ID,
  Source ID, exact code, faithful paraphrase, optional short quote, precise location, grade/band,
  subject/domain, population, evidence type, confidence, notes, SSLS 3.0 relevance. Never merge
  separate requirements because they co-occur in a paragraph.
- **§19 Crosswalk rules** — dimensions: MA content standard; WIDA standard / key language use /
  language expectation / proficiency consideration; NGESL practice; ELE Blueprint priority; CASEL
  competency + integration practice; Learning for Justice domain/outcome; BICS/CALP implication;
  UDL consideration; MTSS component; special-population guidance; grade band; setting; observable
  learner evidence; educator action; possible SSLS 3.0 implication. Relationship classes: exact
  alignment, strong conceptual, partial, complementary, tension/conflict, no demonstrated
  relationship, insufficient evidence. Two-sided citations + rationale on every row; never
  manufacture alignment.
- **§22 Research framework document contents** — executive summary; purpose/boundaries; research
  questions; source-selection criteria; authority hierarchy; terminology and population
  definitions; methodology; framework-by-framework findings; grade progression; content-language
  integration; SEL/academic integration; equity and justice analysis; special-population analysis;
  BICS/CALP analysis; convergences/differences/tensions/gaps; SSLS 3.0 implications; unresolved
  questions; limitations; bibliography; register appendices.
