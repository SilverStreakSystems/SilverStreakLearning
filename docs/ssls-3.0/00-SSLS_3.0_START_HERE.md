## 1. The idea in one minute

SSLS means Silver Streak Learning Systems, the complete modular ecosystem.
Silver Streak Learning is the instructional and educator-facing platform within that ecosystem.
They are related, but they are not interchangeable.

**North Star**

> Make Learning Visible.

**Operational meaning**

> Make learning, expectations, steps to success, and effort visible.

SSLS 3.0 exists to develop a governed learning-systems ecosystem in which evidence related to learning can remain understandable, traceable, and actionable by accountable humans without losing the context needed to interpret it responsibly. The project seeks to reduce the distance between expectations and evidence, evidence and interpretation, interpretation and reasoning, reasoning and decision, decision and next action, effort and its learning context, implementation and validation, and evidence and any claim made from it.

This is project direction, not evidence that SSLS has already achieved it.

## 2. What "visible" means

Visibility is not surveillance and it is not a demand to quantify everything.

| Dimension | What should become understandable | What it must not become |
|---|---|---|
| Learning | What a learner understands, can do, explain, create, apply, or demonstrate, with relevant context | A single score standing in for the learner |
| Expectations | What success requires, what goal or standard applies, and what evidence would demonstrate it | Hidden criteria or moving targets |
| Steps to success | Where the learner is going, what comes next, what supports are available, and how progress can be recognized | A rigid automated prescription |
| Effort | Work, persistence, strategies, practice, revision, and relevant support conditions | A proxy for mastery, behavior, worth, motivation, or compliance |

Effort and achievement remain separate constructs. Visible effort may help explain a learning process. It does not prove mastery. Mastery does not prove how much effort was required.

## 3. What SSLS 3.0 is trying to preserve

File 01 defines the enduring commitments. At entry-point level, remember seven things:

- **Evidence** — what was actually observed or recorded.
- **Reasoning** — why a decision was made.
- **Conditions** — supports, constraints, independence conditions, and limitations.
- **Expectations** — what success means.
- **Steps to success** — what comes next and why.
- **Effort** — relevant work, strategies, practice, persistence, and revision.
- **Human judgment** — the accountable person remains the decision-maker.

The system may organize, explain, surface, and assist. It does not replace authorized human judgment in consequential decisions.

Visibility must serve understanding. It must not become surveillance, behavioral tracking for its own sake, unnecessary data collection, an effort-based judgment of worth, or opaque automated decision-making.

## 4. SSLS 3.0 is a successor, not a rename

SSLS 3.0 does not automatically inherit SSLS 2.0.

Earlier work may contain useful evidence, ideas, lessons, designs, implementation history, or failed approaches. It does not become current SSLS 3.0 authority merely because it existed before or would be convenient to reuse.

When predecessor material is considered:

1. preserve its original identity and provenance;
2. determine what it actually says or demonstrates;
3. compare it with current SSLS 3.0 authority and evidence;
4. preserve conflicts rather than silently merging them;
5. recommend ADOPT, ADAPT, SUPERSEDE, ARCHIVE, REJECT, or UNRESOLVED where appropriate;
6. obtain the required human decision before treating predecessor content as controlling.

A recommendation is not a disposition until it is accepted through the governed process.

## 5. Keep the kinds of truth separate

SSLS 3.0 keeps these states distinct:

Vision → Research → Candidate → Requirement → Approved Decision → Planned → Implemented → Tested → Released → Public Claim

Each transition needs its own basis.

A draft is not approval.
A requirement is not implementation.
Implementation is not validation.
A passing test is not release authorization.
Deployment is not automatically release.
A screenshot is not accessibility or security proof.
AI-assisted review is not independent validation.

When the evidence stops, the claim stops.

## 7. Recover current truth before substantial work

Before substantial work:

1. read File 04;
2. check its verification timestamp and evidence cutoff;
3. determine whether its status is fresh enough for the task;
4. re-verify dynamic facts when necessary;
5. read File 05 to confirm authorization;
6. identify the controlling source;
7. identify predecessor evidence being used;
8. identify missing decisions, requirements, evidence, or implementation prerequisites;
9. surface unresolved conflicts before execution.

Do not reconstruct current status from memory, chat history, old dashboards, filenames, screenshots, or predecessor implementation.

### 7A. Authority in one minute

File 02 contains the full hierarchy. For orientation only:

Owner decision → canonical governance → accepted bounded decisions/specifications → approved requirements → explicitly promoted predecessor content → verified primary external sources → qualified research → unpromoted predecessor evidence → working material and implementation convenience

Only governed internal authority creates permission.

Primary sources and research can strongly inform decisions without becoming internal SSLS authority. Code, tests, prototypes, screenshots, deployments, matrices, crosswalks, PDFs, prompts, and AI output can provide descriptive or working evidence without becoming approval.

When sources disagree, first ask whether the conflict is about what *should* be true or what actually *is* true. File 02 controls the resolution.

## 8. Evidence and source discipline

File 02 controls source authority, source classes, research-source quality, evidence-quality tests, promotion, and conflict resolution.

At entry-point level:

- `[C]` **Confirmed** — directly supported by identified evidence appropriate to the proposition.
- `[I]` **Supported interpretation** — a reasoned interpretation with its basis stated.
- `[U]` **Unknown/unverified** — not established.
- **PROPOSED** — candidate direction awaiting the required decision.

Keep three questions separate:

1. Is the source authoritative for SSLS governance?
2. Is the source good research evidence for this question?
3. Is the evidence valid descriptive evidence of what SSLS actually did?

Those are different tests.

Research evidence does not substitute for validation evidence. Implementation evidence does not create permission. A working matrix, crosswalk, prototype, prompt, workbook, repository file, or self-labeled "SSOT" does not become governing authority by appearance or repetition.

Unknown is valid. Unknown never grants permission.

## PART II — END-TO-END DEVELOPMENT ROUTING

## 9. Development lifecycle

The lifecycle below is a routing model. It is not a current phase model, maturity model, gate system, roadmap, or authorization.

Intent → Research → Problem Definition → Candidate Concept → Decision → Requirements → Design → Architecture → Implementation Planning → Build → Integration → Verification → Validation → Findings → Evidence → Release Preparation → Release Decision → Operations → Research Feedback

Work may move backward as evidence changes. Research may reopen requirements. Testing may reopen design. Findings may reopen decisions. The lifecycle is directional, not strictly linear.

## 10. Lifecycle routing table

| Work domain | Core purpose | Expected output | Route detailed control to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intent | Clarify why the work is being considered | objective, problem question, constraints | File 01 / File 06 |
| Research | Reduce uncertainty with evidence | sources, findings, limitations, recommendation | research artifacts / File 02 |
| Problem definition | State the problem without prematurely choosing a solution | problem statement, assumptions, falsifiers | File 06 |
| Candidate concept | Develop explicit alternatives | options, tradeoffs, risks, dependencies | File 06 / decision preparation |
| Decision | Establish authorized direction | recorded owner decision | active decision register |
| Requirements | Convert direction into testable obligations | requirements and acceptance criteria | active requirements register |
| Experience design | Define understandable human interaction | flows, states, content, interaction specifications | future design artifacts |
| Architecture | Define coherent technical boundaries and dependencies | architecture decisions/specifications | future architecture artifacts |
| Data & authorization | Define records, purpose, access, and constraints | data/authorization specifications | future data/security artifacts |
| Implementation planning | Make authorized work executable without inventing policy while coding | task packet, dependencies, tests, rollback | requirements + repository instructions |
| Build | Implement the authorized change | code/configuration/content | repository evidence |
| Integration | Verify components and external boundaries work together | integrated implementation and test evidence | requirements + test artifacts |
| Verification | Check specified behavior | executed checks and results | active evidence/claims register |
| Validation | Determine whether the intended requirement is satisfied | validation evidence and findings | active evidence/claims register |
| Findings | Record defects, gaps, or unresolved observations | finding record | active evidence/claims register |
| Evidence | Preserve what was observed, how, and with what limitations | evidence record | active evidence/claims register |

| Research feedback | Feed operational evidence back into learning and development | revised questions, requirements, decisions | appropriate controlling source |

File 00 maps the work. It does not become the detailed procedure for every row.

## PART III — CONTINUOUS RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENT

## 11. Research is continuous

File 01 establishes continuous R&D as an enduring SSLS 3.0 commitment. File 02 controls how research sources are qualified.

Research is not a one-time step before building. It may inform problem definition, learning design, product strategy, accessibility, privacy, security, authorization, interoperability, architecture, technical feasibility, standards, predecessor disposition, burden, and sustainability.

Every material research effort should distinguish:

1. what the source or observation explicitly establishes;
2. the SSLS 3.0 interpretation;
3. the resulting recommendation;
4. what remains unknown;
5. what decision, experiment, requirement, or validation could reduce uncertainty.

Research informs authority. It does not create authority.

## 12. R&D questions before tools

A material R&D effort should start with a question, not a technology. Examples:

- Can expectations be made clearer without increasing documentation burden?
- Can steps to success be visible without becoming an automated prescription?
- Can effort be represented without becoming a behavioral score?
- Can support conditions remain attached to learning evidence without making capture impractical?
- Can a deterministic workflow solve the problem before AI is introduced?
- Can commodity capability be integrated rather than rebuilt?

Technology exploration without a question has a remarkable ability to become six weeks of extremely sophisticated wandering.

## 13. Prototype discipline

Use precise language for prototypes.

| Prototype type | Useful for | Does not prove by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Concept sketch | idea structure | usability, implementation |
| Clickable prototype | interaction concept | backend behavior, security, conformance |
| Technical spike | narrow feasibility question | production architecture |
| Data prototype | data-shape or transformation concept | legal authorization or production retention design |
| Authorization prototype | candidate access logic | independently validated security |
| Vertical slice | selected end-to-end behavior | full-product readiness |
| Synthetic simulation | fictional scenario behavior | real-world effectiveness or adoption |

Prototype success is evidence about the prototype, not production readiness. Detailed experiment methods belong in future research or testing artifacts when authorized.

## 14. AI R&D boundary

Prefer deterministic and explainable workflows before AI where they can solve the problem adequately.

Before proposing AI, ask:

- What problem requires probabilistic behavior?
- What new uncertainty does the model introduce?
- What data would enter the model?
- What data must never enter it?
- What human approval remains required?
- What happens when the model is wrong?
- Can the output be explained and challenged?
- Can generated content be distinguished from verified content?
- Can the workflow fail safely if the AI service is unavailable?

AI assistance remains subordinate to governed human judgment.

## PART V — IMPLEMENTATION HANDOFFS

## 16. Standard handoff packet

For substantial work handed to ChatGPT, Claude, Lovable, GitHub, or another implementation surface, include:

- objective;
- governing sources;
- current state;
- target state;
- scope;
- exclusions;
- predecessor evidence;
- decisions already recorded;
- applicable requirements;
- acceptance criteria;
- unknowns;
- dependencies;
- data boundary;
- authorization boundary;
- accessibility requirements;
- requested deliverable;
- validation method;
- evidence required;
- mutation boundary.

A handoff should make it harder, not easier, for the receiving tool to invent missing authority.

## 17. Implementation-readiness check

Before material implementation, confirm that the task has enough upstream definition to proceed. At minimum ask:

- Is current status fresh enough?
- Is the work authorized?
- Is the controlling decision identified?
- Are applicable requirements identified?
- Are acceptance criteria explicit?
- Are predecessor dependencies known?
- Are data and authorization implications understood?
- Are accessibility requirements explicit where human-facing?
- Are dependencies identified?
- Is validation defined?
- Is evidence retention defined?
- Is reversibility or rollback understood?
- Would implementation require inventing a role, module, schema, integration, or technology choice?

If key answers are missing, the right outcome may be more research, a decision, or a requirement rather than code.

## PART VI — PRACTICAL WORKING RULES

## 18. Practical preflight

Before substantial work, answer:

1. **Outcome** — What exact result is requested?
2. **Authority** — Which source controls the question?
3. **Current truth** — What does current evidence establish?
4. **Scope** — Is the work authorized?
5. **Predecessor** — Does it depend on SSLS 2.0 or earlier material?
6. **Research** — What uncertainty should be reduced first?
7. **Terms** — Are File 07 definitions being used?
8. **Unknowns** — What is not established?
9. **Dependencies** — What decision, requirement, evidence, or implementation must exist first?
10. **Acceptance** — What would count as successful completion?
11. **Validation** — How will the result be checked?
12. **Evidence** — What record will prove the check, for which version/environment, and with what limitations?
13. **Mutation** — Does the task commit, merge, deploy, publish, change visibility, invite collaborators, use real data, or change an external system? If so, verify exact authorization first.

## 19. Traceability

For material governed work, preserve:

Requirement → Decision → Task → Implementation → Validation → Finding → Evidence → Claim

File 01 establishes traceability as an enduring commitment. File 02 determines what sources and evidence may support each link. Not every piece of work requires every node. Do not invent missing nodes merely to make the chain look complete.

A future reader should be able to answer:

- Why was this work done?
- Who authorized it?
- What requirement did it satisfy?
- What changed?
- How was it checked?
- What failed?
- What evidence exists?
- What may now be claimed?
- What remains unknown?

## 20. What a useful result leaves behind

Where applicable, a strong SSLS 3.0 result makes clear:

- intended outcome;
- governing authority;
- research basis;
- current versus target state;
- predecessor provenance;
- assumptions and unknowns;
- conflicts;
- recommendation versus approval;
- requirements;
- dependencies;
- acceptance criteria;
- implementation changes;
- validation;
- findings;
- evidence and limitations;
- downstream propagation needs;
- next authorized action.

More documentation is not automatically better governance. A new artifact, field, approval step, or control should identify the decision it improves, the risk it reduces, or the evidence it preserves.

## 21. Stop conditions

Stop rather than invent authority when:

- two sources appear to control the same concept;
- a required governing source cannot be located;
- current status is too stale to support the action;
- authority would have to be inferred from placement, naming, recency, polish, "canonical," "SSOT," gate, or maturity labeling;
- predecessor material would have to be treated as current without authorization;
- a requirement, decision, role, permission, schema, module, integration, technology selection, or implementation state would have to be invented;
- research evidence would have to masquerade as validation evidence;
- a matrix, crosswalk, prototype, AI output, repository artifact, screenshot, or deployment would have to be treated as approval;
- real or potentially identifying data appears where unauthorized;
- a credential or secret is exposed;
- a claim exceeds its evidence;
- an AI system would have to approve or validate its own work;
- an irreversible or external action lacks explicit authorization;
- the proposed validation method cannot support the requested conclusion.

A blocked action can be a correct project outcome.

## 22. End-to-end completion questions

Before describing material work as complete, ask:

1. What problem did this address?
2. What research or evidence supported doing it?
3. What decision authorized the direction?
4. What requirement defined success?
5. What was implemented?
6. What changed from the prior state?
7. How was it verified?
8. What validation was performed?
9. What findings remain?
10. What evidence was retained?
11. What claim, if any, is now supportable?
12. What claim remains unsupported?
13. What must be revisited if a dependency changes?
14. What next authorized work follows?

If these cannot be answered, the project may have activity without end-to-end traceability.

## 23. The entry-point test

A competent person or AI system entering SSLS 3.0 should quickly be able to answer:

1. What is SSLS 3.0?
2. What are we trying to make visible?
3. What source controls my question?
4. What is actually true right now?
5. What am I authorized to do?
6. Where does research fit?
7. How does an idea become a requirement?
8. How does a requirement become implementation?
9. How is implementation verified and validated?
10. What evidence is retained?
11. What happens when a finding contradicts the plan?
12. What must remain a human decision?
13. How does work move toward release without implying release?
14. How does operational evidence flow back into R&D?
15. Where do I go next?

If this document makes those answers harder to find, it has failed as an entry point.
