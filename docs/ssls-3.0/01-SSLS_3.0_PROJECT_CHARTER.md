## Purpose

Establish the durable constitutional boundary for SSLS 3.0: what the program is, why it exists, what principles constrain it, how predecessor material is treated, who holds decision authority, what AI may never decide, and how the charter itself may change.

## 1. Project identity

Silver Streak Learning Systems 3.0, abbreviated SSLS 3.0.

The identity distinction is foundational:

| Term | Meaning | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| SSLS | The complete Silver Streak Learning Systems modular ecosystem | Never used as a synonym for a single platform |
| Silver Streak Learning | The instructional and educator-facing platform within the SSLS ecosystem | Never used as the name of the full ecosystem |

Terminology is controlled by File 07. If File 07 changes either definition through an authorized process, this charter must be checked for consistency.

## 2. North Star

> Make Learning Visible.

**Operational meaning**

> Make learning, expectations, steps to success, and effort visible.

The concise North Star remains the enduring identity statement. Its operational meaning identifies four distinct dimensions that SSLS should preserve throughout research, product development, design, implementation, and evaluation:

- **Learning** — what a learner understands, can do, explain, create, apply, or demonstrate, with relevant context.
- **Expectations** — what success requires, what goal or standard applies, and what evidence would demonstrate it.
- **Steps to success** — the path forward, next useful actions, available supports, and recognizable progress without turning the learner into an automated prescription.
- **Effort** — relevant work, persistence, strategies, practice, revision, and support conditions where they serve an authorized learning purpose.

Effort and achievement remain separate constructs. Effort does not prove mastery; mastery does not prove how much effort was required. Effort must not become a proxy for behavior, worth, motivation, compliance, or a blended achievement score.

The North Star means SSLS should help authorized people understand learning through evidence, expectations, context, reasoning, support conditions, steps forward, effort, and limitations that remain inspectable.

It does not authorize:

- surveillance;
- behavioral monitoring for its own sake;
- engagement optimization as a substitute for learning;
- unnecessary collection;
- reduction of a learner to a single score;
- opaque automated conclusions;
- expansion of scope merely because data or technology makes it possible.

The North Star is directional. It is not an implementation claim, product requirement, or authorization to collect data.

## 3. Project purpose

SSLS 3.0 exists to develop a governed learning-systems ecosystem in which evidence related to learning can remain understandable, traceable, and actionable by accountable humans without losing the context needed to interpret it responsibly.

The project seeks to reduce the distance between:

- expectations and evidence;
- evidence and interpretation;
- interpretation and reasoning;
- reasoning and decision;
- decision and next action;
- effort and its learning context;
- implementation and validation;
- evidence and any claim made from it.

The aim is not to make everything measurable. The aim is to make the right things understandable.

This purpose establishes direction. Detailed product hypotheses, target users, jobs, capability areas, and outcome measures belong in File 06 and remain subject to evidence and owner decision.

## 4. Successor boundary

SSLS 3.0 is a governed successor, not a rename, migration label, refactor label, or automatic continuation of SSLS 2.0.

No predecessor artifact, decision, requirement, schema, role, route, module, integration, technology choice, phase model, maturity result, implementation state, validation result, or public claim carries forward merely because it existed previously.

Predecessor material may be:

- preserved as historical evidence;
- researched for useful ideas;
- proposed for bounded promotion;
- adapted into new SSLS 3.0 candidate work;
- superseded or archived through an authorized decision.

Every use must preserve provenance, original identity, and lineage. No AI system may silently relabel, merge, overwrite, or promote predecessor material.

The formal disposition vocabulary for predecessor artifacts must be defined in File 07 before this charter is canonicalized. Until then, Project Instructions may guide working behavior but do not themselves confer canon status on predecessor content.

## 5. Founding governance model

SSLS 3.0 follows these enduring rules.

1. **Human authority is explicit.** Owner approval is a human act, not a system state inferred by software.
2. **One controller per concept.** Competing governing sources are a defect that must be reconciled.
3. **Default deny.** Absence, ambiguity, mismatch, stale evidence, or unresolved authority never grants permission.
4. **Evidence before claim.** Every material claim is bounded by what its evidence actually supports.
5. **States remain separate.** Vision, research, candidate, requirement, approved decision, planned, implemented, tested, released, and public claim are not interchangeable.
6. **Normative truth and descriptive truth remain separate.** What should happen is different from what currently exists.
7. **Narrower and safer wins on unresolved ambiguity.** Conservative interpretation is preferred until authority resolves the question.
8. **Provenance is preserved.** History is retained; supersession does not erase its predecessor.
9. **Reversibility is preferred.** When evidence does not justify an irreversible choice, prefer the option that is easier to unwind.
10. **Burden must earn its place.** A governance step must name the decision it improves, the risk it reduces, or the evidence it preserves.
11. **Explainability outranks opaque convenience.** Deterministic, inspectable processes are preferred before probabilistic or autonomous ones.
12. **Maturity is reported honestly.** Approval, implementation, validation, release, adoption, and compliance are different propositions and must never be collapsed.
13. **Traceability is material infrastructure.** Important work should be traceable through the chain from obligation or need to decision, task, implementation, validation, finding, evidence, and claim.
14. **Unknown is an acceptable governed outcome.** A system that refuses to invent certainty is behaving correctly.
15. **Research remains open to disconfirmation.** SSLS should seek evidence that could prove a favored idea wrong, not merely evidence that makes it look reasonable.
16. **Learning visibility does not justify data maximalism.** The North Star remains bounded by purpose, privacy, proportionality, and human dignity.
17. **Authority, research quality, and descriptive evidence remain distinct.** A source may be excellent research without creating SSLS authority; implementation evidence may describe what exists without proving it was authorized. File 02 controls the detailed distinctions.
18. **Research evidence and validation evidence remain distinct.** Evidence that informs a design or requirement does not substitute for evidence that an identified SSLS implementation satisfies that requirement.

## 6. Evidence and traceability commitment

SSLS 3.0 treats evidence as a governed object, not decorative support added after a conclusion.

Material evidence must be usable to answer:

- what proposition it supports;
- what object and version it concerns;
- how it was produced;
- in which environment;
- by whom or by what process;
- when it was produced;
- whether the method is relevant to the proposition;
- what limitations apply;
- whether the evidence is independent;
- when re-verification is required.

The intended traceability spine is:

Requirement → Decision → Task → Implementation → Validation → Finding → Evidence → Claim

Not every task will instantiate every node. No node may be invented merely to make a chain look complete.

File 02 controls source authority, research-source quality, evidence-quality tests, promotion, and conflict resolution. The active registers identified by File 03 control decision, requirement, evidence, and claim records.

A working matrix, crosswalk, prototype, prompt, repository artifact, screenshot, deployment, PDF/export, AI output, or self-labeled "SSOT" may provide working or descriptive evidence. None becomes governing authority merely through existence, repetition, polish, implementation, or naming.

## 7. Continuous research and development commitment

Research and development is continuous across SSLS 3.0 rather than confined to a single pre-build stage. Research may inform problem definition, learning design, product strategy, accessibility, language access, privacy, security, authorization, interoperability, architecture, technical feasibility, performance, maintainability, standards, predecessor disposition, burden, and sustainability.

Material research should distinguish what a source or observation establishes, the SSLS 3.0 interpretation, the recommendation that follows, what remains unknown, and what decision, experiment, requirement, or validation could reduce uncertainty.

Research informs authority; it does not create authority. Research evidence does not substitute for validation evidence. Prototype or experiment success proves only what its method and object can support.

## 8. Human decision authority

The project owner is the final human authority for SSLS 3.0 governance decisions.

Software and AI may support decision preparation, but they may not replace the authorized human decision-maker.

Any consequential decision about a learner or other person must remain with a qualified human who holds the relevant legal, professional, or organizational authority for that decision. SSLS may organize evidence, surface constraints, explain rules, or draft options; it must not autonomously determine eligibility, placement, grading, discipline, safety action, medical or legal outcomes, employment action, or equivalent consequential outcomes.

This principle is enduring and cannot be weakened by implementation convenience.

## 9. AI-assistance boundary

AI is governed assistance.

AI may support:

- research;
- synthesis;
- drafting;
- restructuring;
- requirements and specification proposals;
- architecture and design proposals;
- code generation;
- test generation;
- critique and gap identification;
- option generation;
- documentation;
- evidence organization.

AI may **not**:

- approve its own work;
- create owner authority by implication;
- establish canon;
- record a human decision as though it made that decision;
- waive a control;
- close a risk or finding without the required authority and evidence;
- promote predecessor material;
- authorize a public claim;
- represent simulated review as independent review;
- claim compliance, certification, conformance, adoption, readiness, or effectiveness without supporting evidence and authorization;
- make a consequential decision for a person;
- commit, merge, deploy, publish, release, change visibility, or invite collaborators unless the exact action is explicitly authorized.

When multiple AI systems disagree, resolve the disagreement through authority and evidence, not model consensus. Claude-generated or Claude-assisted work must be identified as such. Cross-model critique is AI-assisted review, not independent validation.

## 10. Educational and human-centered commitments

These are founding design constraints, not claims of current implementation or effectiveness.

1. **Human judgment remains accountable.** The system supports professional reasoning; it does not replace it.
2. **Grade-level intellectual demand is preserved.** Language development or disability does not, by itself, justify lowering the intellectual target.
3. **Constructs remain separate.** Language development, literacy, disability-related need, content knowledge, support conditions, and grade-level expectation are not collapsed into one explanatory axis.
4. **Support conditions travel with performance evidence.** What a learner did cannot be interpreted responsibly without knowing what support was present.
5. **Scaffolds and required access supports are not conflated.** Temporary instructional scaffolds may have fading logic; required accommodations and assistive technology are not treated as temporary merely for convenience.
6. **Negative constraints are visible.** The system should make clear what the evidence does not justify inferring.
7. **Learners are not reduced to composite scores.** A single blended score must not replace construct-specific evidence.
8. **Visibility must serve learning, not surveillance.** Evidence collection must remain tied to an authorized purpose.
9. **Expectations should be understandable.** Where appropriate, learners and accountable adults should be able to understand the intended learning, relevant success criteria, and what evidence could demonstrate success.
10. **Steps to success should be understandable.** The next useful action should be visible without becoming a rigid automated sequence.
11. **Effort may be visible without being weaponized.** Relevant effort may help explain learning, but must not become a character judgment, compliance score, substitute for mastery, surveillance metric, or mechanism for comparing learners' worth.
12. **The learner remains larger than the record.** SSLS records evidence about learning; the record is not the person.

Detailed educational requirements require separate approved requirements and validation.

## 11. Enduring privacy, security, accessibility, and trust commitments

### 11.1 Privacy

- collect only what an authorized purpose requires;
- prefer synthetic and non-identifiable data during development and evaluation unless a later governed expansion explicitly authorizes otherwise;
- never treat pseudonymization as equivalent to synthetic data;
- do not place student data into an AI model training path;
- preserve purpose limitation and minimum-necessary use.

### 11.2 Security

- authorization must be explainable and enforced below the presentation layer;
- hidden interface elements never constitute access control;
- least privilege and default deny are founding constraints;
- no credential, secret, token, or key is treated as acceptable project content.

### 11.3 Accessibility

- accessibility is an architectural and design constraint from the beginning;
- interaction must be designed for keyboard use, semantic structure, screen-reader compatibility, visible focus, sufficient contrast, responsive behavior, cognitive accessibility, reduced motion where appropriate, language access, and recoverable errors;
- brand expression never overrides access or truthful state communication;
- a design commitment is not a conformance claim.

### 11.4 Trust and explainability

- consequential system behavior should be understandable enough for an accountable human to interrogate;
- derived states should be reconstructable from inputs, rules, and evidence where practicable;
- limitations accompany conclusions;
- automation should not hide uncertainty.

Specific requirements, standards targets, test methods, and conformance decisions belong in their controlling artifacts.

## 12. Modularity and architecture boundary

SSLS is intended as a modular ecosystem, but modularity is a design direction, not proof that any module exists.

A future capability may qualify as a module only through governed definition of its purpose, authorized users, workflows, canonical records, dependencies, exclusions, access rules, implementation boundary, evidence, and maturity.

This charter does not create:

- modules;
- routes;
- schemas;
- roles;
- permissions;
- tenancy;
- integrations;
- services;
- repositories;
- technologies;
- deployment environments.

Architecture and implementation must not be backfilled into governance merely because a prototype, prior version, or external tool already contains them.

## 13. Interoperability boundary

SSLS may integrate with external systems where future requirements justify doing so, but it should not silently become the authoritative system of record for information governed elsewhere.

External standards, identifiers, and interfaces inform design only when they are current, relevant, and appropriately adopted. Adopting a standard as a design target does not itself establish conformance.

## 14. End-to-end development commitment

SSLS 3.0 should preserve traceability across the full development lifecycle without confusing the lifecycle with authorization. A useful routing model is:

Intent → Research → Problem Definition → Candidate Concept → Decision → Requirements → Design → Architecture → Implementation Planning → Build → Integration → Verification → Validation → Findings → Evidence → Release Preparation → Release Decision → Operations → Research Feedback

This is an enduring development orientation. It is not by itself a program phase model, maturity model, gate system, roadmap, or work authorization. Detailed procedures and current permissions belong in their controlling sources. Research and operational evidence may reopen earlier work; the lifecycle is iterative rather than strictly linear.

## 15. Program-level non-goals

SSLS 3.0 is not intended to become:

- a surveillance or engagement-optimization system;
- an autonomous decision-maker for consequential human outcomes;
- a mechanism for reducing professional accountability;
- a justification for staff reduction, wage suppression, or shifting institutional burden to learners or families;
- a replacement for an external assessment authority merely because SSLS can store related evidence;
- a second uncontrolled source of truth for records already governed elsewhere.

Product-specific non-goals belong in File 06. Current implementation and release status belong in File 04.

## 16. Program success dimensions

Program success is evaluated through separate dimensions rather than a blended readiness score.

| Dimension | Founding question |
|---|---|
| Learning visibility | Can authorized people understand learning, expectations, steps to success, effort, relevant conditions, and limitations without collapsing distinct constructs? |
| Governance and artifact integrity | Is the project understandable, internally consistent, traceable, and governed by explicit authority? |
| Implementation maturity | Does an authorized capability actually exist and behave as specified? |
| Evidence confidence | How strongly has the relevant proposition been verified, by what method, with what limitations? |
| Burden and sustainability | Does the system and its governance return enough value to justify the effort required to use and maintain them? |

A strong result in one dimension does not erase a failure in another. Safety-, privacy-, accessibility-, authorization-, or evidence-critical failures are not averaged away.

Thresholds and measurement methods require separate decisions and requirements.

## 17. Decision and change discipline

Founding principles change only through explicit owner decision.

A valid charter amendment must:

1. identify the exact charter version being changed;
2. state the proposed change;
3. state why the change is needed;
4. identify affected artifacts and decisions;
5. assess safety, scope, claims, data, accessibility, and burden impacts where relevant;
6. preserve the superseded version;
7. record the owner decision through the governed decision mechanism;
8. update File 03 status and lineage;
9. update dependent artifacts;
10. update File 04 only if the amendment changes current status.

No amendment occurs by:

- chat consensus;
- AI inference;
- a newer filename appearing;
- implementation drifting away from the charter;
- a prototype becoming popular;
- repeated use of an unapproved term;
- storage location.

## 18. Separation of current state from enduring policy

This charter contains no current-state block. Questions such as these are answered elsewhere:

- What is authorized right now? → File 04 and File 05.
- What artifacts currently exist? → File 03 plus verified storage/repository evidence.
- What repository, branch, code, schema, or deployment exists? → File 04 plus direct descriptive evidence.
- What phase or maturity has been achieved? → File 04.
- What claims are currently authorized? → active evidence/claims register identified by File 03.

If a sentence in this charter becomes true or false merely because the project advanced a week, it probably belongs in File 04 instead.
